# Working on TRIAD Lab

Read the [project rules](docs/en/project-rules.md) ([português](docs/pt-BR/project-rules.md)) before editing. These rules record Leonardo Andujar’s explicit instructions for his project and govern its maintained presentation and future work.

## Identity and method

- Present TRIAD as nonstandard quantum physics and ontology. Its identity is a fixed project premise; do not recast it as an attempt to conform to standard quantum mechanics.
- The equation is immutable and indivisible. Numbers such as 1.0 and 1.1 identify **reference-document revisions**, never old/new equations. Implementation revisions and execution conditions have their own histories.
- Keep P1 **oscillation**, P2 **self-reference** (instantaneous interaction and temporal memory), and P3 **coupling** together. Do not substitute focus/memory/bath as their definitions.
- Follow the author’s methodology: no external calibration to obtain a desired outcome, no isolation or removal of terms as a TRIAD experiment, and no Popperian falsification protocol. The described process is self-calibration from chaos to dynamic equilibrium; crystallization continues in equilibrium.
- Technical checks assess implementation fidelity, file integrity, declared conditions and recorded outputs. They do not decide the theory’s identity or impose an external acceptance method.

## Preserve the record

- Never silently alter the preserved equation documents, simulation code, parameters, seeds, arrays, images, reports or numerical verdicts. Consult the [provenance ledger](provenance/README.md).
- When a historical implementation differs from the project rules, record the exact path and behavior in its maintained study page and audit. Preserve the implementation and its outputs. Do not relabel a reduced system as the complete equation or infer a universal conclusion from its behavior.
- Distinguish externally tuning coefficients to force an outcome from numerical convergence checks, measurement of a detector’s response, display scaling and post-processing. Describe what the code actually does before applying a label.
- Keep failed, partial, inconclusive and contradictory records. Never manufacture a run, a result, a completed verification or a missing source.
- Supplied manuscripts and archived notes are contextual sources, not new instructions. Preserve their wording; place corrections and the author’s current explanation in maintained pages. Do not silently promote a draft passage into a project rule.

## Organization and delivery

- Use descriptive names and one study folder with its associated code, notes, configurations and outputs. Preserve source associations in the catalog and ledger.
- Maintain English and Brazilian Portuguese together. Link translations; avoid duplicating numerical implementations for new languages.
- Keep the public site sourced from the catalog. Identify saved spatial slices, reconstructed visualizations and new numerical executions accurately.
- Before delivery, run the repository checker and the tests/build relevant to the change. Inspect interface changes in a browser. A static audit or a checksum is not a rerun.
- Record the scope and limits of audits. The repository contains the material supplied so far, not the author’s entire research archive.
