CBs 10996 is the key interval text; UET Vll 74 (Ur) is the retuning text; the gods' numbers exist independently; McClain's thesis fits Just ratios into the sexagesimal system, but the text does not declare musical intent. The evidence is concrete in the tablets and in metrology. The rest is strong correlation, not demonstration that the scribes thought "Anu = octave".

CBs 10996 and the tuning texts (Ur and Nineveh)

CBs 10996 (Nippur, Neo-Babylonian copy, probably from an older original) lists 14 string pairs (dichords) numbered 1 to 7. The logogram SA = string / interval / mode. Odd (primary) pairs: 4ths and 5ths — used to generate the seven tunings. Even pairs: 3rds and 6ths — probably checks.

Standard reconstruction (Kilmer and later):

- 1-5 nis tubri
- 7-5 serum
- 2-6 isartum
- 1-6 salsatum
- 3-7 embubum
- 2-7 rebutum
- 4-1 nid qablim
- 1-3 isqum
- 5-2 qablitum
- 2-4 titur qablitum
- 6-3 kitmum
- 3-5 titur isartim
- 7-4 pitum
- 4-6 zerdum/serdum

UET Vll 74 (Ur, Old Babylonian, ~1800 BC) gives the retuning cycle of the 9-string sammu. Strings 8 and 9 are octaves of 1 and 2. Each section: "if it is in mode X, interval Y is 'unclear' (tritone), change string Z → now it is in mode W". The seven canonical modes: isartum, kitmum, embubum, pitum, nid qablim, nis tubri, qablitum.

This is a heptatonic diatonic system generated by 4ths/5ths, predating Pythagoras. The residual debate is direction (ascending vs. descending) and whether the 3rds carried a slight temperament. The text needs no cosmology to work: it is an instrument manual. Nineveh/Assyrian texts (KAR 158 and similar) classify hymns by the same mode names. The vocabulary is stable from Old to Neo-Babylonian.

Gods' numbers vs. the 2:1, 5:4, 4:3 ratios

Established fact: Anu = 60, Enlil = 50, Ea/Enki = 40, Sin = 30, Samas = 20, Istar = 15. The numbers replace the god's name in texts. Anu as 60 coincides with the base of the positional system.

Ratios McClain highlights:

- 60:30 = 2:1 (octave)
- 60:40 = 3:2 (5th)
- 40:30 = 4:3 (4th)
- 50:40 = 5:4 (major 3rd)
- 60:50 = 6:5 (minor 3rd)
- 50:30 = 5:3 (major 6th)

The 40-50-60 trio yields the 4:5:6 chord. Ea is patron of music and wisdom; the instrument's 4th string is called "Ea made it" in UET Vll 126.

What the tablet does not say: "Anu's number is the octave because...". The numbers exist in god lists and cryptographic substitutions independently of the musical texts. The match with Just ratios in the base-60 system is exact and uses exactly the integers a sexagesimal scribe would use to generate the principal intervals with the least arithmetic work.

First-principles conclusion: the correlation is real and nontrivial. Buying McClain's whole thesis (gods = tones, ziggurats = harmonic matrices, extension to Plato and Genesis) requires extra premises the tablets do not support. The usable core is: the same numerical set that organizes the pantheon also organizes the ratios the tuning texts actually employ.

Ziggurat plans and metrology

Units:

- Standard cubit (ammatu / kus): ~49.5–50 cm (30 fingers). Variations: Nippur cubit (~51.8 cm on the bronze rod, debated interpretation), "large cubit" ~75 cm.
- Nindan (rod): 12 cubits ~ 6 m in the later system.
- 1 iku of area = 100 square nindan in the old system, or later equivalents.
- Etemenanki (Babylon, Esagil tablet): base 15 nindan × 15 nindan ~ 90–91.5 m (archaeology confirms ~91.5 m). Declared height equals the base → ideal cube. Seven stages in the text; real height probably lower (mudbrick statics). Round sexagesimal numbers and an area of 1 iku.
- Ziggurat of Ur (Etemennigur, Ur-Nammu): base ~62.5 × 43 m. First stage ~11 m tall. In 50 cm cubits that approaches 125 × 86 (or 120 × 80 if rounded). Not a cube; a rectangle with practical + symbolic proportions. Three stages, triple staircase on the NE side.

The recurring pattern is: dimensions in multiples of 12, 15, 30, 60; sometimes 3-4-5 triangles in the layout; numbers that also appear in the gods and in reciprocal tables. Metrology is at once technical (brick, ramp, drainage) and cosmographic (house of the foundation of heaven and earth). No need to force a musical reading to explain the plans; the same numbers were already in the measurement system.

What can be tested without dogma: take the 60:50:40:30 ratios, generate the Just scale, compare with the CBs 10996 dichords and with the nindan/cubit dimensions of the two best-documented ziggurats. The overlap exists at the arithmetic level. The explicit "this is music" intention is not written down. [garbled in the converted source]
