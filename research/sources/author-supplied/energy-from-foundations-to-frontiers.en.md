How to read this study
Energy is a physical quantity that allows describing states, transformations and transfers between systems. Science has very well tested theories to calculate it in numerous regimes. Questions about its relation to gravity, vacuum, information and the origin of the Universe remain open. There is no single formula that replaces all energy theories. Feynman: conservation · Noether, 1918 · Tong: energy and gravity
The request "everything about energy" crosses practically all natural sciences. This report offers a broad map of the main fields and a verifiable route through the literature. It is not an inventory of all published articles, a systematic review of each subarea or a demonstration that no other theory exists.
Scope and assumptions. Text in Portuguese for a reader interested in mechanisms and observables, without requiring university training in physics. Update cutoff: 6/9/2026. Global coverage, with sources mostly in English. "Current" means a result located and checked up to that cutoff; it does not guarantee coverage of every preprint published in the period.
Four levels of reading the evidence: measurement is the signal obtained by the instrument; result is its estimate accompanied by conditions and uncertainty; interpretation is the explanation within a model; hypothesis is a possibility still under investigation. One article may contain all four levels.

1. The vocabulary that organizes the field
Energy is not a substance with a universal composition. It is a quantitative property of a physical state and of its interactions. Its usefulness appears when very different transformations can be related by the same balance: falling, heating, electricity, chemical reaction and light emission. Feynman: conservation

| Quantity | What it expresses | Typical unit |
|---|---|---|
| Energy E | Quantity associated with the state and its transformations | joule, J |
| Power P | Rate of energy transfer or conversion | watt, W = J/s |
| Frequency f | Number of cycles per second | hertz, Hz = 1/s |
| Force | Rate of change of momentum; mechanical interaction | newton, N |
| Temperature T | Parameter of thermal equilibrium and distribution of states | kelvin, K |
| Entropy S | Thermodynamic quantity linked to accessible states | J/K |
| Energy density u | Energy per volume | J/m³ |
| Energy flux | Energy crossing area per time | W/m² |

The units connect: 1 J = 1 kg·m²/s²; 1 kWh = 3.6 million joules; 1 eV = 1.602176634 × 10⁻¹⁹ J. The electron-volt is convenient in atomic and nuclear physics. Kilowatt-hour is energy, even though it contains "watt" in the name. BIPM: International System
Worked example: an appliance receiving 100 W continuously for ten hours receives 1 kWh. Another receiving 1,000 W for one hour receives the same energy, in a shorter interval. These examples are illustrative arithmetic, not measurements of specific appliances.
The school definition "capacity to perform work" is useful as a start, but needs a condition: not all available energy can be fully converted into useful work. A thermal reservoir may contain much energy and offer little work capacity relative to an environment at almost the same temperature. Thermodynamics describes this difference. Feynman: thermodynamics

2. Physical forms and their connections
The categories below are ways of accounting for energy in different theories and scales. They are not independent species of a substance. When adding them, it is necessary to avoid counting the same contribution twice. Feynman: conservation · Feynman: field energy · Dürr et al., 2008

| Form or context | Expression or description | Where it appears |
|---|---|---|
| Translational kinetic | E = mv²/2, in the non-relativistic regime | Vehicles, molecules, fluids |
| Rotational kinetic | E = Iω²/2, rigid body | Turbines and flywheels |
| Gravitational potential | mgh locally; -GMm/r for two masses | Falls, orbits and reservoirs |
| Elastic | kx²/2 for a linear spring | Deformations and vibrations |
| Electrostatic | Charge configuration and electric field | Capacitors, ions, bonds |
| Magnetic | Field and configuration of currents/magnetization | Inductors, motors and materials |
| Radiant | Electromagnetic fields; hf per photon | Light, radio, X and gamma rays |
| Internal | Set of microscopic degrees of freedom | Thermodynamics of matter |
| Chemical | Differences between electronic and molecular states | Fuels, batteries, metabolism |
| Nuclear | Differences of nuclear binding energy | Fission, fusion and radioactivity |
| Rest | E₀ = mc² | Particles and composite systems |
| Relativistic gravitational | Depends on the geometry and the conditions of the problem | Gravitational waves and cosmology |

The first expressions hold in the indicated approximations. Gravitational energy mgh ceases to suffice for large altitude changes; mv²/2 ceases to suffice near the speed of light. More general models recover these formulas in the appropriate limits. Feynman: work and potential · Feynman: relativity
Heat and work describe transfers. A body has internal energy; heat is energy transferred due to a temperature difference. Mechanical, electrical or other work describes other transfer mechanisms. The distinction depends on the chosen description and the system boundary. Feynman: thermodynamics
Solar, wind and geothermal are names of sources; electricity and hydrogen are vectors or forms of transport and use. "Renewable" characterizes replenishment of the resource on a relevant time scale, not a new physical law. Storage conserves part of the energy supplied earlier for later use. IEA: solar and wind integration · IEA: hydrogen 2025 · DOE: pumped hydro

5. Statistics, entropy and free energy
Statistical mechanics relates many microscopic states to macroscopic properties. In the canonical ensemble, in equilibrium with a bath at temperature T, probabilities follow Boltzmann weights. The partition function Z normalizes the distribution. Feynman: statistics · Tong: statistical physics
F is the Helmholtz free energy. For fixed temperature and volume, under the usual equilibrium conditions, it allows deciding between states. The Gibbs free energy, G = U + pV - TS, is convenient for fixed temperature and pressure. "Free" refers to thermodynamic availability under constraints, not to gratuitous supply. Feynman: thermal potentials · IUPAC: Gibbs energy
Why does the energy minimum not settle everything? A state may cost more energy, but have many more microscopic ways of occurring. The entropic term changes which state predominates. Moreover, barriers can keep a system in a metastable state for a long time. A diamond, a charged battery or a protein need not be at the simplest global minimum to persist. Identifying stability requires specifying time and transformation paths. Feynman: statistics · IUPAC: activation
The chemical potential μᵢ measures how free energy changes when adding matter of a species, keeping the appropriate variables. Differences of chemical and electrochemical potential guide diffusion, ion transport and reactions. A thermodynamically favourable reaction may be slow because of its activation barrier. IUPAC: chemical potential · IUPAC: activation
Negative temperatures are a real special case. Systems prepared with a bounded-above energy spectrum may present inverted populations and a negative statistical temperature. Braun and collaborators realized such a state with ultracold atoms in an optical lattice. This does not mean obtaining an ordinary body "colder than zero kelvin": the distribution is inverted and transfers energy to a positive-temperature system when the relevant coupling is established. Braun et al., 2013

6. Frequency, vibration and resonance
Frequency describes temporal repetition. Energy also depends on amplitude, effective mass, stiffness, number of excitations and couplings. For a harmonic oscillator of amplitude A:
Two oscillators at the same frequency may have different energies. Keeping mass and frequency, doubling the amplitude quadruples the energy. In sound waves, intensity also depends on amplitude and the properties of the medium. Therefore, an isolated frequency does not inform how much energy is present or will be absorbed. Feynman: oscillator · Feynman: modes
For a photon, energy is hf. For N photons of the same frequency, the excitation energy is Nhf. The per-quantum formula and the classical-oscillator formula answer different descriptions. There is no contradiction: the classical regime can emerge from many quanta occupying modes. Planck, 1901 · Feynman: equipartition
Resonance increases the response when the drive meets a favourable dynamics of the system. A swing receives energy from the push; a circuit receives energy from the source; a material absorbs radiation according to its spectrum and coupling. The accumulated energy results from the source's work. Dissipation, saturation, nonlinearities and detuning limit the real response. Feynman: resonance

8. Mass, matter and binding energy
In special relativity, the general relation for a particle of mass m and momentum p is:
At rest, E₀ = mc². For a massless particle, E = pc. Kinetic energy depends on the reference frame; the invariant mass of a system depends on its total energy in the zero-total-momentum frame, including interactions and internal motions. Feynman: relativity · Einstein, 1905
Binding energy is a difference. If the mass of a bound system is less than the sum of the masses of the separated components, the difference corresponds to the energy released in formation, when the other products and fluxes are included. Separating the system requires returning energy. That is why chemical and nuclear reactions change masses, although on very different scales. Rainville et al., 2005 · LLNL: NIF results
Rainville and collaborators compared atomic mass differences and gamma-ray energies in nuclear processes. The 2005 work provides a direct test of mass-energy equivalence, for the processes and precision of the experiment. The report consulted abstract and metadata, not the full article. Rainville et al., 2005
Where does the mass of protons and neutrons come from? It is not enough to add quark masses as if they were loose spheres. Strong-interaction dynamics contributes decisively to hadron masses. Dürr and collaborators computed hadron masses in lattice QCD and obtained agreement with observations within the assessed uncertainties. This links "mass of matter" to an interacting field theory. Dürr et al., 2008
Chemistry is also energy physics. Bonds, electronic repulsions, molecular states, solvation and product distribution enter the balance. Breaking an isolated bond costs energy; a reaction may release energy because product formation and their interactions compensate that cost. The complete thermodynamic description uses free-energy differences and chemical conditions. IUPAC: Gibbs energy · IUPAC: chemical potential · Alberty and Goldberg, 1992
The use of expressions like "converting matter into energy" is an abbreviation. The concrete analysis tracks which particles, fields and final states appear, their energies and their momenta. Energy remains a quantity assigned to those states. Einstein, 1905 · Feynman: relativity

9. Materials: bands, phonons and collective
phases
In solids, the behaviour of many electrons and atoms produces properties that cannot be deduced by treating each component as isolated. Electronic bands organize the accessible states; phonons describe collective lattice-vibration excitations; defects and interfaces alter transport and losses. Tong: solids

| Research object | Energy question | Applications and limits |
|---|---|---|
| Electronic bands | Which states conduct or absorb light? | Semiconductors and photovoltaics |
| Phonons | How do vibrations transport and scatter energy? | Heat, acoustics and insulation |
| Excitons and polaritons | How do electronic excitations couple to light? | Optical capture and cavity devices |
| Superconductors | How does a zero-DC-resistance transport phase arise? | Magnets and circuits, with operating limits |
| Electrochemical interfaces | How do ions and electrons cross materials? | Batteries, electrolysers and corrosion |

The first three rows are descriptions of degrees of freedom and collective models, not new primary energy sources. Cavity devices and batteries show how engineering these states modifies rates, stability and useful output. Tong: solids · Hymas et al., 2026 · Sang et al., 2026
The 1957 work of Bardeen, Cooper and Schrieffer established a microscopic theory of superconductivity with electronic pairing. It explains an important class of materials. The field includes other pairing and correlation regimes; the mere existence of a theory does not mean all materials are explained by the same mechanism. Bardeen, Cooper and Schrieffer, 1957
Superconductivity reduces one dissipation channel, but does not supply energy by itself. Equipment may require refrigeration, control power and dissipating components. The usefulness of a magnet or circuit depends on limits of temperature, current, field, material and facility architecture. Bardeen, Cooper and Schrieffer, 1957 · Hu et al., 2026
Transversal frontier: integrating properties simultaneously. A material may conduct very well but be unstable; store much energy but degrade; absorb light but recombine charges before extraction. The tandem and solid-state papers discussed below are examples of research at these interfaces. Zhang et al., 2025 · Sang et al., 2026

Energy systems may be nonlinear, chaotic and collective. The difficulty is not reduced to finding a total energy: it is predicting how it circulates between scales and degrees of freedom. 11. Energy in life
Organisms maintain structures, gradients and processes through continuous matter and energy exchanges. Metabolism combines reactions, transport and catalysis. The energy availability of a reaction depends on concentrations, temperature and chemical conditions, besides its standard value. Alberty and Goldberg, 1992 · Mitchell, 1961
R is the gas constant and Q′ represents the activity quotient in the biochemical convention used. For ATP, pH, magnesium, ionic strength and ATP/ADP/phosphate ratios matter. ATP works as a coupling intermediate; it does not contain a universal "packet" of energy extractable independently of the cellular environment. Alberty and Goldberg, 1992
The mechanistic chain is concrete. Electronic transport may establish an electrochemical gradient across a membrane. Proton passage through ATP synthase couples that gradient to molecular rotation and ATP synthesis. The chemiosmotic mechanism proposed by Mitchell in 1961 connected processes that previously lacked a unified explanation. Mitchell, 1961
Itoh and collaborators provided an especially direct demonstration in 2004: mechanically imposed rotation on F₁-ATPase drove ATP synthesis. It is an experimental bridge between mechanical work and chemical storage. The component's performance must not be extrapolated to the efficiency of a whole organism. Itoh et al., 2004
Photosynthesis. Light energy is absorbed by pigment complexes and may feed excitation transfer, charge separation and chemical reactions. In 2023, Li and collaborators demonstrated correlated absorption and emission of individual photons in a natural LH2 antenna complex. The experiment observes one stage of the process, without following the whole path to carbon fixation. Li et al., 2023
Mind and consciousness. Neural activity has costs and biophysical mechanisms; but a quantity of joules, by itself, does not specify the content of a thought or provide a measure of consciousness. Identifying a cognitive function requires additional observables and a model connecting activity, organization and behaviour. This is a conceptual distinction: the energy balance does not replace functional description.

12. Origin of life and active matter
An energy question about the origin of life is: which flows could feed reactions, maintain gradients and sustain organization before modern cells existed? Answering requires reconstructing plausible chemical mechanisms and distinguishing laboratory demonstration from historical reconstruction. Hudson et al., 2020
Hudson and collaborators built a microfluidic reactor with a pH gradient and precipitates containing iron/nickel/sulphur. In it, CO₂ and H₂ participated in formate formation. Isotopic labelling supported identifying the carbon's origin; changes to components and gradient altered production. The result demonstrates a chemical route fed by specific conditions. Hudson et al., 2020
This does not demonstrate a complete cell nor determine where life arose. Between forming an organic molecule and obtaining replication with heredity and evolution there are additional steps. Energy models need to connect synthesis, compartmentalization, maintenance and reproduction without presupposing that the last step follows automatically from the first.
England derived thermodynamic limits associated with self-replication, relating growth, durability, entropy and heat. These results make some constraints of reproduction quantitative. They do not establish that all matter exposed to energy will become alive, nor that all evolution maximizes dissipation. England, 2013
Active matter is an experimental family in which components consume energy locally and produce motion or tension. Sanchez and collaborators used microtubules and ATP-fed kinesin motors to form networks with persistent collective flows and motions. The behaviour emerges from the interaction of fed components. Sanchez et al., 2012

| Observed result | Sustained interpretation | What remains open |
|---|---|---|
| Gradient-driven reaction | Environmental source can feed chemistry | Historical sequence to life |
| Collective motion in motor network | Local consumption can organize flow | General principles of organization |
| Replication heat limit | Replicating requires resources under defined hypotheses | Open evolution in built systems |

The convergence of these lines lies in investigating connected systems: organization, environment and dissipation must appear in the same model. Structural persistence requires both adequate energy and a dynamics capable of using it. Sanchez et al., 2012 · England, 2013

Toyabe and collaborators demonstrated that feedback based on information about a particle can guide work extraction from a thermal bath. Information allows choosing the action; the extracted energy has a physical origin. The global balance includes measurement, control and memory. Toyabe et al., 2010
Free energy in ML and active inference. In variational inference, a function named free energy can be written as KL divergence minus log-evidence. In its probabilistic form, it is not automatically measured in joules. Equivalence with thermodynamic energy depends on an explicit physical mapping. The Friston formalism and collaborators discuss dynamical systems and inference under particular conditions; it does not turn any loss function into physical energy. Friston et al., 2022
For a computing architecture, it is worth measuring separately mathematical cost, energy per task, latency and result quality. An algorithm may reduce operations and still consume more energy if it increases data movement, execution time or infrastructure. This comparison requires the energy boundary of the device and the facility. IEA: energy and AI 2026

14. Thermodynamics and quantum batteries
At the quantum scale, coherence and correlations alter which transformations and charging rates are possible. Brandão and collaborators formulated families of free energies constraining transformations under specified thermal operations. The generalization adds operational conditions; it does not remove the costs of resources used in preparation. Brandão et al., 2015
Stored energy and extractable work differ. Ergotropy is the maximum work extractable by unitary operations within a given model. Its value depends on the state and the Hamiltonian. Preparing the state, performing the operations and restoring the controls are additional parts of the complete process. Hu et al., 2026 · Brandão et al., 2015

15. Quantum vacuum and negative energy
The vacuum of a field theory is a physical state. Its definition depends on fields, conditions and, in curved geometry, the relation between spacetime and observers. Energy values must be treated with renormalization and appropriate references. Hollands and Wald, 2015
Casimir effect. Configurations of bodies and fields may present forces associated with the geometry dependence of energy. Lamoreaux measured the force between conducting surfaces in a 1997 experiment. This provides evidence of a measurable interaction; it does not alone determine the absolute energy of the cosmological vacuum. Lamoreaux, 1997
A cycle using surface approach to obtain work must include the cost of restoring the configuration and any change of materials or environment. The interpretation of an accessible infinite reservoir does not follow from observing a force. The cycle balance remains the adequate question. Lamoreaux, 1997 · Feynman: thermal ratchet
Negative energy has several meanings. A negative potential energy may merely reflect the choice of zero and a bound state. Negative renormalized energy density in quantum field theory is another statement, relative to a reference state. Pointwise classical conditions may be violated; quantum inequalities restrict duration, magnitude or averages in specific classes. Feynman: work and potential · Kontou and Sanders, 2020
These results sustain research on energy conditions and spacetime. They do not by themselves provide a controllable macroscopic negative-mass material, nor a demonstration of warp propulsion or traversable wormholes. The passage from a mathematical solution to physical construction requires sources and stability compatible with the theory itself. Kontou and Sanders, 2020
Cosmological constant problem. The gravitating energy of the vacuum and cosmic acceleration raise a deep difficulty: explaining the observed value without an apparently delicate combination among contributions. The popular figure of "120 orders of magnitude of error" depends on the cutoff and hypotheses used in the estimate; it is not a single experimental comparison independent of the theoretical procedure. Martin, 2012
This area contains proven phenomena, solid formalisms and problems still unsolved. Keeping them distinct allows researching the vacuum without reducing all discussion to a generic confirmation or rejection. Hollands and Wald, 2015 · Martin, 2012

17. Dark energy: evidence and
2026 update
Dark energy is the name given, in cosmological models, to the component used to describe accelerated expansion. It is not synonymous with dark matter. In the fluid parametrization, w = p/ε is defined. The cosmological constant has w = -1; dynamical models may admit variation. The microscopic nature remains unknown. Martin, 2012 · DESI DR2 IV, 2026
The DESI DR2 IV analysis, submitted in July 2026 and updated in August, added information from the full shape of Lyman-alpha forest correlations. This new measurement shifts the central estimate toward ΛCDM, although preferences persist for a varying-dark-energy parametrization when different data are combined. DESI: Lyα update

| Data in the 2026 paper | Reported preference for w₀wₐCDM over ΛCDM |
|---|---|
| DESI | 1.9σ |
| DESI + cosmic microwave background (CMB) | 2.7σ |
| DESI + CMB + DES-Dovekie supernovae | 3.2σ |
| DESI + CMB + updated Pantheon+ | 3.0σ |
| DESI + CMB + Union3.1 | 3.5σ |

The values depend on the combination and the model w(a) = w₀ + wₐ(1 - a). They are not direct probabilities that a theory is true. The paper contains a small editorial inconsistency: one passage of the conclusion says 3.1σ, while abstract, specific discussion and table 4 indicate 3.2σ for DES-Dovekie. The table above uses the explicit combination. DESI DR2 IV, 2026
What does this result change? The narrative of ever-increasing evidence does not adequately describe the update: the new information may bring parameters closer to the cosmological-constant model. At the same time, the residual preference motivates testing models and new observational combinations. DESI: Lyα update
What does it not resolve? It does not identify a particular scalar field, does not demonstrate that dark energy can be collected by a device, and does not alone determine the final fate of the Universe. Cosmological constant, dynamical fields, gravitational alterations and systematic effects require their own quantitative comparisons. Martin, 2012 · DESI: Lyα update
This is an example of a frontier where measurement can be very precise and the fundamental interpretation remains open.

18. Black holes and quantum

Hawking showed that quantum fields on a black-hole background can produce thermal emission. For the ideal Schwarzschild case:
M is the mass and A is the horizon area. These relations connect gravity, thermodynamics and quantum theory. The result is semiclassical: spacetime is treated geometrically while the fields are quantized. Hawking, 1975
The theory predicts that emission removes energy and may reduce the black hole's mass. Astrophysical Hawking radiation is not presented in this study as an established direct observation. Analogue experiments in material media may test aspects of kinematics and horizon mechanisms, but their balance belongs to the built platform. Hawking, 1975 · Hollands and Wald, 2015
Information in black holes. The combination of quantum evolution, horizon and evaporation raises questions about information recovery and the microscopic description of entropy. The available formalisms do not equal an experimentally confirmed theory of all quantum-gravity regimes. Hollands and Wald, 2015 · Maldacena, 1997/1998
Donoghue showed how to treat general relativity as an effective quantum theory at low energies. Certain long-distance corrections can be computed even without knowing the whole extreme-energy theory. "Effective theory" means scale-organized validity, not mere approximation without predictive content. Donoghue, 1994
Maldacena proposed a relation between gravitational theories and field theories in specific contexts, known as AdS/CFT. It offers deep tools for studying gravity, quantum states and information. Geometry and conditions matter: it is not enough to transfer each result directly to observed cosmology. Maldacena, 1997/1998

In engineering, the complete path goes from resource to conversion, transport and use. An advance in one stage may lose advantage in another. The measurement boundary must accompany any announced efficiency or density. IEA: solar and wind integration · DOE: flow batteries

Batteries use electrochemical differences; capacitors store electric energy; flywheels use rotation; elevated reservoirs use gravity; thermal systems store sensible or latent heat; hydrogen and synthetic fuels carry chemical energy supplied in production. 20. Solar, batteries and hydrogen
Perovskite-silicon tandem. Layers with different spectral responses allow harvesting light in more than one junction. Zhang and collaborators reported in 2025 a 1 cm² cell with certified 33.15% efficiency, using silicon interface structures on industrially textured silicon. Zhang et al., 2025
The same work presented stability tests under defined conditions, including 91.7% retention after a thousand hours at 80 °C under illumination in nitrogen. Sodium versus lithium. The Yao, Benson and Chueh study modelled competitiveness paths. Sodium abundance alone does not determine system cost: energy density and materials quantity per stored energy also matter. Competitiveness trajectories are scenarios, not a universal property of any sodium battery. Yao, Benson and Chueh, 2025
Solid-state, 2026 example. Sang and collaborators obtained, in one condition, 85% retention after 350 cycles at 1 C and 2.5 MPa, with 3 mAh/cm² areal loading. Earth's balance. Climate depends on the difference between absorbed and emitted radiation, and its redistribution. The CERES programme observes radiative fluxes; heat storage, mainly oceanic, helps relate the imbalance to system warming. Cloud, aerosol, gas and circulation variations participate in the balance. 24. How to measure energy and interpret
a paper
There is no single instrument for all contexts. Measurement connects signals to a model: temperature rise, voltage and current, velocity, radiation spectrum, particle counting or interferometer deformation. Inference quality depends on instrumental response and uncertainties. NIST: energy measurement · NIST: uncertainty · LIGO/Virgo, 2016

| Context | Observables | Conversion and cautions |
|---|---|---|
| Electric | Voltage and current over time | Integrate v(t)i(t); include phase, transients and auxiliaries |
| Calorimetric | Temperatures, masses, flux and thermal properties | Sensible/latent heat, losses and parallel reactions |
| Mechanical | Force, displacement, torque and velocity | Work and power, with losses and stored energy |
| Optical | Spectrum, flux and detector response | Energy per photon and photon number |
| Nuclear | Products, spectra and counts | Detection efficiency, background and reaction identification |
| Cosmological | Distances, redshift and correlations | Inference conditioned on the cosmological model |

The table is an analysis guide, supported by the measurement types of the cited studies, not a universal protocol. Feynman: field energy · NIST: energy measurement · Chen et al., 2025 · DESI DR2 IV, 2026
The denominator may change the conclusion. Wh/kg must state which masses were included. Efficiency must state which inputs were measured. Fusion gain must identify capsule, target, plasma or facility. "Faster" must state which task was performed with equivalent quality. Sang et al., 2026 · Zhang et al., 2025 · LLNL: NIF results · IEA: energy and AI 2026
Dynamics and average. An intense power peak may carry little energy if it lasts very briefly. A transient output may consume previously accumulated energy. An average result may hide fluctuations that make use unfeasible; distributions may also reveal interesting mechanisms. Feynman: resonance · Jarzynski, 1997 · Barato and Seifert, 2015
Uncertainty is part of the result. It specifies the interval and reliability of the estimate under the measurement model. Repetition, comparison between methods and documentation of conditions help distinguish effect, artefact and interpretation. No conclusion should depend on erasing an unexpected result merely because the initial model did not predict it. NIST: uncertainty
To deepen a paper, the useful sequence is: question → system and connections → observables → balance → result → explanatory hypothesis → limitations → next experiment. This is a reading route proposed by this report, not a physical theorem.
