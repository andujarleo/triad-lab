What this list contains, reduced to what can be demonstrated in the text — no forge legend, no "Pythagoras heard the cosmos", no Hz.

What is operational fact

None of these authors measures frequency. They measure string-length ratios (or, in later discourse, ratios of "movements"). If tension and density are constant, modern frequency is the inverse of the length ratio. That is physics; it is not in their vocabulary.

There are two distinct objects the tradition mixes:

1. Instrument tuning (tetrachord, lyre octave).
2. Cosmological construction using the same ratios.

Plato does (2). Philolaus and Archytas are closer to (1), with a leap to the cosmos. Nicomachus and Iamblichus are already reception.

Philolaus (fr. 6a) — the minimum core

Text transmitted by Nicomachus and Stobaeus. Practice vocabulary (hypate, mese, trite, neate), not laboratory.

| Interval | Name in the fragment | Ratio |
| --- | --- | --- |
| syllaba | fourth | 4:3 |
| di' oxeian | fifth | 3:2 |
| dia pasan / harmonia | octave | 2:1 |
| epogdoos | tone | 9:8 = (3:2)/(4:3) |
| diesis | remainder of the fourth after two tones | 256:243 |

Explicit arithmetic consequence in the fragment:

- octave = 5 × (9:8) + 2 dieses
- fifth = 3 × (9:8) + 1 diesis
- fourth = 2 × (9:8) + 1 diesis

This already contains the problem that never goes away: (9:8)^6 > 2:1. Six Pythagorean tones exceed the octave. The difference is the comma. Philolaus does not "solve" it; he describes the diatonic scale practice already used and names the residual. Cosmic harmony in Philolaus (limited + unlimited joined) is analogy with that structure. Not empirical derivation from the sky.

Archytas — the first system attempting all three genera

Three means (fr. 2):

- Arithmetic: a − b = b − c. Ex.: 12, 9, 6 → 12:9 = 4:3 (fourth), 9:6 = 3:2 (fifth)
- Geometric: a/b = b/c
- Harmonic (subcontrary): the part by which the greater extreme exceeds the mean is the same part by which the mean exceeds the lesser. Ex.: 12, 8, 6 → 12:8 = 3:2, 8:6 = 4:3

Decisive theorem: between terms in superparticular ratio (n+1):n there is no rational geometric mean.

So octave, fifth, fourth, and tone do not split in half in equal ratio with integers. That kills the idea of a "true semitone" as √9:8.

Tetrachord divisions (Ptolemy, Harm. I.13), all closing 4:3; the lowest interval is the same in all three genera (28:27):

| Genus | Ratios (high → low) |
| --- | --- |
| Diatonic | 9:8 · 8:7 · 28:27 |
| Chromatic | 32:27 · 243:224 · 28:27 |
| Enharmonic | 5:4 · 36:35 · 28:27 |

Difference from Philolaus/Plato: Archytas's diatonic is not 9:8 · 9:8 : 256:243. He privileges superparticular ratios and the means, not tuning by stacked fifths/fourths.

There is also the acoustic fragment: pitch tied to speed/force of motion. Physical hypothesis, not measurement.

Plato, Timaeus 35a–36d — world soul, not lyre manual

The Demiurge's procedure:

1. Mixes Being, Same, Other.
2. Cuts portions: 1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 9, 27 (powers of 2 and of 3 — the lambda).
3. Inserts arithmetic and harmonic means → 3:2, 4:3, 9:8 appear.
4. Fills each 4:3 with 9:8 tones; what remains is 256:243.
5. Cuts the strip in half, crosses it in an X, bends it into two circles (Same / Other); the interior divides into seven planetary circles following the double and triple intervals.

The resulting scale is Philolaus's Pythagorean diatonic, extended over several octaves. Not evidence that planets "sound" these ratios. It is a numerical generator Plato identifies with the order of cosmic thought and, later, with the orbits.

What the text does not do: calibrate ears, describe enharmonic/chromatic genus, or assert Hz.

Timaeus 53c–55c — another layer

Here the object is the world's body, not the soul.

- fire: tetrahedron
- air: octahedron
- water: icosahedron
- earth: cube
- the whole: dodecahedron

The link between solids is geometric proportionality and triangular faces, not the 1-2-3-4-8-9-27 series. Treating 35a–36d and 53c–55c as the same "musical code" collapses two constructs. Plato juxtaposes them; he does not identify them.

Euclid, Sectio Canonis — axiom + monochord

Structure: physical prologue + propositions in the Elements' style + practical division of the canon.

Prologue (what he assumes):

- without stroke and motion, silence;
- pitch correlated with density of impacts;
- relations between sounds = ratios of integers;
- consonances must be multiple (n:1) or superparticular ((n+1):n).

Results the treatise wants to demonstrate:

- octave = 2:1
- fifth = 3:2
- fourth = 4:3
- tone = 9:8
- the tone does not divide into two equal ratios (same Archytas theorem)

Internal limit, already visible in the criterion itself: octave + fourth = 8:3 is consonant to the ear and is neither multiple nor superparticular. Ptolemy will use exactly that against Pythagorean dogma. The Sectio chooses the criterion and then discards the datum that wounds it.

The last propositions describe the linear division of the string. That is the tradition's experimental instrument — not the hammer legend.

Ptolemy, Harmonics — the only one refusing both dogmatisms

Method position:

- Pythagoreans: correct ratio, ear treated as noise;
- Aristoxenians: sovereign ear, ratio treated as alien to the object.

Ptolemy wants both: monochord to test ratios; ear to decide what is actually melodic. He prefers superparticulars but does not accept the rule "only multiple or epimoric = consonance" when the phenomenon (8:3) refutes it.

Book III shifts again to soul, virtues, and zodiac. There the text stops being instrumental harmony and returns to cosmic analogy. Separating Books I–II from III is the sound reading; merging them is not.

Nicomachus, Enchiridion + Iamblichus, Life of Pythagoras

Transmission layer, 2nd–4th century.

What they are good for:

- citing Philolaus 6a;
- systematizing means and the seven→eight-string scale;
- freezing the forge-and-weights narrative.

What they are not good for as 6th–5th-century sources:

- the hammer story is physically false (hammer weight does not determine pitch as the legend requires; what matters is the bar/anvil or the string);
- Iamblichus is Neoplatonic hagiography. Good for mapping how the myth was assembled, not what Archytas measured.

Using Nicomachus as a witness for Philolaus: yes. Using him as a witness for the historical Pythagoras: no.

Aristoxenus — the cut preventing romanticization

The science's object: the voice's continuum in pitch space, organized into melodic functions. Instruments: trained ear (akoe) + intellect (dianoia).

Thesis incompatible with the Sectio:

- the fourth holds two and a half tones;
- the tone divides into equal semitones for perception;
- intervals are magnitudes of a continuum, not primarily ratios.

Sharp numerical conflict:

If the pure fourth is 4:3 and the tone is 9:8, the fourth does not contain 2.5 equal tones. Aristoxenus knows he is speaking of another criterion: what the musical ear accepts as "the same step", not what the integer ratio closes.

Without Aristoxenus the list becomes number theology. With him the brute fact remains: the same culture produced two irreducible criteria — rational closure vs. the ear's judgment. Ptolemy is the only one attempting to weld them without pretending one nullifies the other.

What this Greek layer authorizes — and what it does not

Authorizes:

- a small set of ratios stable in string practice: 2:1, 3:2, 4:3, 9:8, and the 256:243 residual;
- a negative theorem: superparticular intervals do not bisect rationally;
- two conflicting ontologies (ratio vs. ear), both Greek, both ancient;
- Plato's use of these ratios as the skeleton of the world soul and the orbits — explicit in the Timaeus, not inferred.

Does not authorize:

- frequencies in Hz, "528", "432", or any absolute calibration;
- identity between Platonic solids and the soul's scale;
- Pythagoras at the forge as experiment;
- that the sky emits the lyre's scale.

The next useful cut, if the corpus continues in the same logic, is no longer Greece: it is what Rome/Boethius freezes and what the medieval monastery inherits as musica mundana [final clause garbled in the converted source].
