# Technical Research Supporting the Two-Channel (BYTE/TOKEN) "Any-to-Any" Architecture with Continuous-Learning Bridge and Field-Equation Anti-Collapse System

## TL;DR
- This report gathers the technical material — mathematical, algorithmic and engineering — supporting the construction of the author's declared architecture, organized in seven fronts (A–G): active inference/free energy for the BYTE ingestion channel; byte-level models and dynamic patching; any-to-any multimodal architectures; methods for assigning and learning online a continuous scalar in [0,1]; the complete mathematics of the anti-collapse field equation (NLS/Gross-Pitaevskii + fractional Laplacian + non-Hermitian term + memory + noise); continuous dynamics as computational substrate; and differentiable engineering.
- The main components have concrete, recent literature grounding: BLT (Meta, 2024) and H-Net (Hwang, Wang & Gu, 2507.07955) for the byte↔patch frontier; L0/hard-concrete (Louizos et al., 2017) and stochastic gates for continuous scalars in [0,1]; de Bouard & Debussche's results that multiplicative noise can prevent/delay collapse in focusing NLS; and Boulenger et al.'s fractional blow-up criteria. All are presented as available technical material, not as definitions of what the homonymous terms mean in the author's project.
- For the numerical and differentiable implementation of the field equation treated as a whole, the material points to symmetric (Strang) split-step Fourier with fractional Laplacian via the |k|^σ multiplier in Fourier space, memory term by sum-of-exponentials approximation (O(1) history recurrence), Euler–Maruyama/Stratonovich stochastic integration, and differentiation by backprop-through-the-solver with checkpointing (torch.fft/JAX, diffrax, torchsde, TorchGPE).

## Key Findings

1. **The BYTE channel by active inference in purely ingestive mode is sustainable in the literature.** The variational free-energy principle (Friston) admits a formulation in which only perception/belief updating occurs (minimization of variational free energy F), separated from action selection (minimization of expected free energy G). A system that only minimizes F, without the policy/action branch of G, corresponds exactly to "ingestion without external output".

2. **The unconventional byte↔token/patch frontier has multiple technical precedents** — entropy patching (BLT), dynamic chunking with learned router and differentiable boundaries (H-Net), boundary predictors, cross-attention between local encoder and global transformer. None of them is a fixed tokenization; all learn the boundaries.

3. **Assigning and learning online a continuous number in [0,1] by "learning"** is directly supported by hard-concrete stochastic gates (Louizos et al.), Gaussian stochastic gates (Yamada et al.), Gumbel-Softmax/Concrete relaxations, and online importance scores (Fisher/EWC, Synaptic Intelligence path-integral, MAS output-gradient). Metrics of "how much was learned" exist: information gain, Bayesian surprise (Itti & Baldi), compression progress (Schmidhuber).

4. **The author's field equation, treated as a whole, is mathematically coherent with a studied family of generalized NLS.** Each term interacts: the focusing cubic nonlinearity Λ|Ψ|² is the engine of collapse (blow-up) governed by the Zakharov–Glassey virial identity; the fractional Laplacian α(−Δ)^(σ/2) alters criticality and dispersion (Laskin; Boulenger et al.); the −iΓ term dissipates/absorbs; V_mem introduces history; and η (noise) can, per de Bouard & Debussche, prevent or delay collapse. Negative and contradictory results exist (conservative noise can *accelerate* blow-up with positive probability) that are preserved here.

5. **Differentiable engineering is viable**: autograd FFT in PyTorch/JAX makes split-step Fourier fully differentiable; the adjoint method (Chen et al., 2018) and checkpointing (Griewank & Walther) allow gradients with controlled memory in long rollouts; TorchGPE already implements a split-step GPE/NLS propagator in PyTorch on top of the autograd FFT stack.

## Details

### A) ACTIVE INFERENCE AND FREE ENERGY (BYTE channel, ingestion)

**Mathematical formulation.** For a generative model p(o,s) and a recognition density q(s), the variational free energy at instant t is
F_t = E_{q(s)}[ ln q(s) − ln p(s, o_t) ] = D_KL[ q(s) ‖ p(s | o_t) ] − ln p(o_t).
Since the D_KL term ≥ 0, F_t is an upper bound on surprise −ln p(o_t) (negative log-evidence). Perceptual inference is q_t = argmin_q F_t. This is the objective sustaining a PURE INGESTION channel: the system updates beliefs q(s) to minimize F without ever needing to emit action.

Active inference adds action selection via **expected free energy** (for a policy π, τ>t):
G(π) = Σ_{τ>t} E_{q(s_τ,o_τ|π)}[ ln q(s_τ|π) − ln p(s_τ, o_τ) ],
with a_t* = argmin_a G(π ∋ a). G decomposes into risk (divergence between expected and preferred outcomes) + ambiguity; equivalently into epistemic gain (information seeking) + pragmatic value. **For the ingestion-only BYTE channel, the G/action branch is omitted; only F (perception/learning) operates.** Learning (parameter optimization) and inference (state optimization) are both minimizations of F; in variational Bayes, self-consistent equations are iterated to convergence, or gradient descent on F is used (Coordinate Ascent Variational Inference / fixed-point iteration is the method used in pymdp).

**Hierarchical generative models and predictive coding.** Predictive coding (Rao & Ballard, 1999, *Nature Neuroscience*; Friston, 2005) organizes the system in a hierarchy of levels, each level generating predictions of the lower level's activity; prediction errors, weighted by **precision** (inverse of variance, "precision weighting"), ascend the hierarchy and drive relaxation and learning. Buckley, Kim, McGregor & Seth (2017), "The free energy principle for action and perception: A mathematical review", derive predictive coding and active inference in continuous time and state — a key reference for continuous low-level flows like a byte channel.

**Predictive coding as an alternative to backpropagation.** Whittington & Bogacz (2017, *Neural Computation*) showed that a predictive-coding network with local Hebbian plasticity approximates backprop; Millidge, Tschantz & Buckley (2022, *Neural Computation* 34(6):1329–1368), "Predictive coding approximates backprop along arbitrary computation graphs" (arXiv:2006.04182); Song, Lukasiewicz, Xu & Bogacz (2020, NeurIPS), "Can the brain do backpropagation? — exact implementation of backpropagation in predictive coding networks". *Critical/negative* result preserved: Zahid, Guo & Fountas (2023), "Predictive Coding as a Neuromorphic Alternative to Backpropagation: A Critical Evaluation" (*Neural Computation* 35(12):1881–1909), critically evaluates the alleged advantages. Song, Millidge, Salvatori et al. (2024, *Nature Neuroscience*), "Inferring neural activity before plasticity as a foundation for learning beyond backpropagation" ("prospective configuration" rule). Millidge, Tang, Osanlouy, Harper & Bogacz (2024, *PLOS Computational Biology* 20(4):e1011183), "Predictive coding networks for temporal prediction" — relevant for time series/raw signals.

**Deep active inference, amortized inference, VAEs, world models.** See Millidge et al.'s survey and "The Free Energy Principle for Perception and Action: A Deep Learning Perspective" (arXiv:2207.06415). Amortized inference (training a recognition network q_φ, as in VAEs) connects active inference to variational autoencoders and world models. Tschantz, Millidge, Seth & Buckley (2023, *PLOS Comp. Biol.*), "Hybrid predictive coding: inferring, fast and slow" (arXiv:2204.02169) — combines amortized (fast) with iterative (slow) inference. Neural Kalman Filtering (Millidge et al., arXiv:2102.10021) for online belief updating.

**Libraries and concrete implementation.**
- **pymdp** (Heins, Millidge, Demekas, Klein, Friston, Couzin & Tschantz, 2022, *JOSS* 7(73):4098; arXiv:2201.03904) — Python, discrete POMDPs, JAX backend, CAVI/fixed-point iteration. Repository: github.com/infer-actively/pymdp; docs: pymdp-rtd.readthedocs.io.
- **RxInfer.jl** (Bagaev, Podusenko & De Vries, 2023, *JOSS* 8(84):5161) — Julia, real-time reactive Bayesian inference via factor graphs and message passing; GraphPPL.jl frontend.
- **ForneyLab.jl** — Forney factor graphs for message passing.
- **ActiveInference.jl** (2024/2025, PMC11765463) — pymdp reimplementation in Julia, allows fitting to empirical data by sampling and variational methods.
- **SPM/DEM** (MATLAB) — original Dynamic Expectation Maximization toolbox inside SPM.
- **cpp-AIF** (Gregoretti, Pezzulo & Maisto, 2024, *Neurocomputing* 568:127065) — multi-core C++.
- Recent hardware-acceleration work: "A Hardware-oriented Approach for Efficient Active Inference" (arXiv:2508.13177, 2025).

For **discretizing/implementing** a continuous byte flow in active inference: use continuous-time predictive coding (Buckley et al. 2017) with generalized coordinates, or a neural Kalman filter (Millidge et al.); in discrete time, pymdp's POMDP scheme with matrices A (likelihood), B (transition), C (preferences — omitted in ingestion mode), D (prior over initial states).

### B) BYTE-LEVEL MODELS AND ALTERNATIVE TOKENIZATION

**Byte Latent Transformer (BLT)** — Pagnoni, Pasunuru, Rodriguez, Nguyen, Muller, Li, Zhou, Yu, Weston, Zettlemoyer, Ghosh, Lewis, Holtzman & Iyer (Meta, 2024), "Byte Latent Transformer: Patches Scale Better Than Tokens" (arXiv:2412.09871; correspondence: artidoro@cs.washington.edu, 16 Dec. 2024). Operates directly on bytes; groups bytes into **variable-size patches** by **next-byte entropy**, estimated by a small auxiliary byte-level language model (100M-parameter transformer, 14 layers, 512 dimension, 512-byte sliding window). When the next byte's uncertainty (entropy) exceeds a threshold, a patch boundary opens — allocating more computation where data are complex. Architecture: lightweight **Local Encoder** (bytes→patches), large **Latent/Global Transformer** over patches, **Local Decoder** (patches→bytes). It is "the first FLOP controlled scaling study of byte-level models up to 8B parameters and 4T training bytes", with BLT matching tokenization-based models (Llama 3) at scale. Open weights: facebook/blt-1b, facebook/blt-entropy (github.com/facebookresearch/blt). 2026 extension: "Fast Byte Latent Transformer" (arXiv:2605.08044) — N bytes → M ≈ N/4 patches.

**H-Net (Dynamic Chunking)** — Hwang, Wang & Gu (2025), "Dynamic Chunking for End-to-End Hierarchical Sequence Modeling" (arXiv:2507.07955). Fully end-to-end differentiable **dynamic chunking** mechanism: a **router module** predicts adaptive per-position boundary probabilities, segmenting into compressed representations; a **smoothing module** interpolates in reconstruction to maintain differentiability; a **residual connection** projects the encoder's initial outputs directly to the dechunking stage, linking local byte-level features to global context. SSM-based encoders/decoders outperform Transformer layers. Flow: Bytes → Encoder → Router → Chunking → Main Network → Dechunking → Decoder. Per the authors, "when matched in compute and data, a one-stage H-Net operating at byte level outperforms a strong Transformer over BPE tokens... matching a token-based Transformer twice its size". On DNA, "each H-Net model reaches the same pre-decay perplexity as the corresponding isotropic model with approximately 3.6× less data" (≈3.6× less data / nearly 4× data-efficiency improvement over isotropic baselines). 2025/2026 variants: **H-Net++** (Zakershahrak & co-author, arXiv:2508.05628) for morphologically rich languages (Persian) — 1.9M-parameter context-mixer, two-level hyper-prior, 0.159 BPB reduction (12% better compression) vs GPT-2-fa; "Adaptive Targeted Dynamic Chunking" (arXiv:2605.30080); "ByteGen" (arXiv:2508.02247, financial data); "Distilling Token-Trained Models into Byte-Level Models" (arXiv:2602.01007).

**Other byte/tokenizer-free.**
- **MegaByte** (Yu et al., 2023) — two-decoder Transformer hierarchy (global over *fixed*-size patches + local autoregressive over bytes); context up to 1.2M bytes.
- **MambaByte** (Wang, Gangavarapu, Yan & Rush, 2024, COLM), "Token-free Selective State Space Model" (arXiv:2401.13660) — Mamba (SSM) at byte level, linear time; outperforms MegaByte on controlled FLOPs. Per the authors, a "speculative decoding adaptation with tokenized drafting and byte-level verification... results in a 2.6× inference speedup over the standard MambaByte implementation" ("This results in a 2.6× inference speedup to the standard MambaByte implementation").
- **SpaceByte** (Slagle, 2024, NeurIPS; arXiv:2404.14408) — partitions bytes into patches aligned to word boundaries; per the author, "on PG-19, SpaceByte reaches bits-per-byte of 1.009... markedly better than MegaByte's 1.083 and the byte-level Transformer's 1.138" (byte-level and MegaByte require ~10× more training FLOPs than subword for comparable performance). Code: github.com/kjslag/spacebyte.
- **MBLM** (arXiv:2502.14553, 2025) — Multiscale Byte Language Models, hierarchy with unlimited stages and different decoders per stage, up to 5M bytes on a single GPU with selective checkpointing.
- **ByT5** (Xue et al., 2022, *TACL* 10:291–306), **CANINE** (Clark et al., 2022) — byte/char encoders with downsampling.
- **bGPT / "Byte Models are Digital World Simulators"** (Wu et al., 2024) — byte modeling as digital-world simulation.
- **Perceiver / Perceiver IO / Perceiver AR** — cross-attention from a latent array to very-high-dimensional inputs, bridging granularities via learned latent bottleneck.
- Curated list: github.com/zjysteven/Awesome-Byte-LLM.

**Bridging granularities (byte↔patch).** The concrete mechanisms used: cross-attention between local encoder (byte) and global transformer (patch); learned pooling; **boundary predictors** (BLT via entropy; H-Net via router with boundary probabilities); differentiable interpolation/smoothing to restore resolution (H-Net). Work on soft tokens/continuous embeddings as inter-level interfaces includes BLT's patch latent representations (arbitrary byte groups mapped to latent representations via lightweight learned encoder/decoder, with no fixed vocabulary).

### C) MULTIMODAL ANY-TO-ANY ARCHITECTURES

Overview (reference survey: "Unified Multimodal Understanding and Generation Models: Advances, Challenges, and Opportunities", arXiv:2505.02567, with 2023–2025 timeline; list: github.com/aidc-ai/awesome-unified-multimodal-models and github.com/any2any-mllm/awesome-any2any).

- **Autoregressive with modality-specific tokenizers → discrete tokens:** **4M** and **4M-21** (Mizrahi/Kar et al., EPFL/Apple; arXiv:2406.09406) — maps dozens of modalities to discrete tokens via modality-specific tokenizers (ViT-based VQ-VAE for image-like modalities; MLP-based discrete VAEs for 3D poses/embeddings), multimodal masking objective, up to 3B parameters and 21 modalities (RGB, edges, SAM, 4DHumans poses, color palettes, metadata, DINOv2/ImageBind features, T5-XXL embeddings); open code at 4m.epfl.ch. **Chameleon** (Meta, 2024, arXiv:2405.09818) — early-fusion mixed-modal with VQ-IMG, causal. **Emu3** (BAAI, 2024, arXiv:2409.18869) — "Next-Token Prediction is All You Need", SBER-MoVQGAN. **AnyGPT** (2024) — EnCodec for audio, SpeechTokenizer for speech, modality-specific prefixes. **Unified-IO 2** (Lu et al., 2024) — structured encoder-decoder with vision/audio/language.
- **AR + diffusion head / diffusion-autoregression mixture:** **Transfusion** (Zhou, Yu, Babu et al., Meta, 2024, arXiv:2408.11039) — predicts the next token AND diffuses images in the same multimodal transformer. **Show-o** (Xie et al., 2024, arXiv:2408.12528) — integrates discrete diffusion schedule. **Bagel/BAGEL** and **Hyper-Bagel** (arXiv:2509.18824, 2025) — unified acceleration framework.
- **Visual-encoding decoupling:** **Janus / Janus-Pro** (DeepSeek, Wu et al., 2025, CVPR) — decouples visual encoding for understanding vs generation.
- **Shared latent space / cross-modal alignment:** **CoDi** and **CoDi-2** (Tang et al., 2023) — composable diffusion generating any combination of modalities in parallel from any combination of inputs. **NExT-GPT** (Wu et al., 2023, arXiv:2309.05519; ICML 2024) — LLM (Vicuna-7B) + ImageBind (6-modality unified encoder) + adapters + diffusion decoders; tunes only ~1% of parameters — "NExT-GPT is tuned with only a small amount of parameter (1%) of certain projection layers" (≈164M of ~12.3B, or ≈1.3%) — plus MosIT instruction tuning (≈5,000 curated dialogues). *Negative/limitation* result preserved: the CoDi authors note it "does not achieve deep human-like reasoning"; NExT-GPT still depends on frozen pre-trained encoders/decoders.
- **Recent 2025/2026:** M2-omni (arXiv, multiple tokenizers/decoders), Emu3.5 ("Native Multimodal Models are World Learners", 2025-10), Uni-X (ICLR 2025, "two-end-separated" for modality conflict), OneCAT, MMaDA, Show-o2 (arXiv:2506.15564), FUDOKI (discrete flow, arXiv:2505.20147).

**Key strategies** relevant to the author's project: (i) universal tokenizers vs modality-specific projections over a shared backbone; (ii) inter-modality routing and multimodal mixture-of-experts; (iii) diffusion mixture (for continuous output) and autoregression (for discrete output). Style note respected: "collapse" in VQ-VAE ("dimensional collapse in VQVAEs", NeurIPS) is a homonymous phenomenon, presented only as reference.

### D) CONTINUOUS [0,1] LEARNING VALUES LEARNED ONLINE

This section gathers the material for the author's LEARNING COMPUTATION — assigning each learning a continuous (explicitly non-binary) number in [0,1] and LEARNING that number online. The methods below are literature material; the name "learning" here does not presume equivalence with the author's concept.

**Continuous gating and continuous relaxations.**
- **L0 regularization / hard-concrete gates** — Louizos, Welling & Kingma (2017), "Learning Sparse Neural Networks through L0 Regularization" (arXiv:1712.01312). Non-negative stochastic gates determine which weights to zero; the **hard-concrete** distribution is obtained by "stretching" a binary concrete and passing through a hard-sigmoid, assigning mass to exactly {0,1} and the rest to (0,1). Concrete formulas:
  s_i = σ( (log u − log(1−u) + α_i)/τ ),  u ~ Uniform(0,1)
  s̄_i = s_i(ζ − γ) + γ,  with typical γ = −0.1, ζ = 1.1, followed by hard-sigmoid clamp in [0,1].
  The α parameters (and temperature β/τ) are trained by gradient together with the network — this is precisely "learning the continuous value online". The gate's expected value is σ(log α − β' log(−γ/ζ)).
- **Concrete / Gumbel-Softmax** — Maddison, Mnih & Teh (2016, arXiv:1611.00712) and Jang, Gu & Poole (2016) — continuous relaxation of discrete variables, the basis of the above gates.
- **Gaussian Stochastic Gates (STG)** — Yamada, Lindenbaum, Negahban & Kluger (2020), "Feature Selection using Stochastic Gates" (arXiv:1810.04247): z_d = max(0, min(1, μ_d + ε_d)), ε_d ~ N(0, σ²), with learned μ_d — a Gaussian clamp in [0,1] with lower variance than hard-concrete. Contextual/conditional extension: arXiv:2312.14254.
- **GRU/LSTM gates and attention weights** as continuous weights in (0,1) via sigmoid/softmax are the classic precedent of learned continuous gating.

**Online estimation of importance/confidence.**
- **Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC)** — Kirkpatrick et al. (2017, *PNAS*; arXiv:1612.00796). Importance Ω_i = diagonal of the **Fisher information matrix** (local loss curvature / Hessian of the KL). Quadratic penalty Σ_i (F_i/2)(θ_i − θ_i*)². *Negative/critical* result preserved: EWC "has consistently shown suboptimal performance"; FIM dependence causes gradient vanishing and inaccurate estimation in certain scenarios (arXiv:2603.18596, "Elastic Weight Consolidation Done Right").
- **Synaptic Intelligence (SI)** — Zenke, Poole & Ganguli (2017, ICML). First-order **path-integral** measure, accumulated along the optimization trajectory (each synapse's contribution to the loss drop) — an online estimator by construction.
- **Memory Aware Synapses (MAS)** — Aljundi et al. (2018). Importance = magnitude of the network-output gradient with respect to weights (squared L2 norm of outputs), computable unsupervised and online.
- **Bayesian online learning / Kalman filtering for weights** — incremental Bayesian updating of the posterior over weights; "Neural Kalman Filtering" (Millidge et al., arXiv:2102.10021) for weights/states.

**Meta-learning of rates and credit assignment.**
- **Hypergradient descent** (Baydin et al., 2018) — learns the learning rate by the loss gradient with respect to the rate itself.
- **Learned optimizers** and **adaptive per-parameter step sizes** — meta-learning of per-parameter steps.
- **Learning to reweight examples** (Ren et al., 2018) and **Meta-Weight-Net** (Shu et al., 2019) — learn a continuous weight per example/sample; **focal weighting** (Lin et al., 2017) — continuous weight per difficulty. These are exactly learned "curriculum/sample weighting" assigning a continuous scalar per item.

**Metrics of "how much was learned".**
- **Prediction gain / information gain** — reduction in loss/uncertainty attributable to an item.
- **Bayesian surprise** — Itti & Baldi (2009, *Vision Research* 49(10):1295–1306, DOI:10.1016/j.visres.2008.09.007, PMCID PMC2782645): "surprise measures how data affects an observer, in terms of differences between posterior and prior beliefs about the world" — formally D_KL[posterior ‖ prior] after observing the data. It is "the strongest known attractor of human attention", with 72% of gaze shifts directed to more surprising than average locations (84% in video). Directly usable as the signal driving the continuous value in [0,1].
- **Compression progress / learning progress** — Schmidhuber (2009), "Driven by Compression Progress…" (arXiv:0812.4360) and "Formal Theory of Creativity, Fun, and Intrinsic Motivation (1990–2010)" (*IEEE TAMD* 2010): intrinsic reinforcement is the *first derivative* of subjective compressibility over time — not the degree of compression itself (avoiding rewarding noise). Latent Bayesian Surprise for curiosity: Mazzaglia et al. (2021, AAAI). *Negative* note preserved: "Interestingness as an Inductive Heuristic for Future Compression Progress" (arXiv:2605.14831) proves that, in general MDPs, it is impossible to estimate learning progress unbiasedly via rewards derived from in-context prediction error; positive result only for the non-temporal subclass (active learning / Bayesian Experimental Design).

### E) MATHEMATICS OF THE AUTHOR'S FIELD EQUATION (all terms together)

The declared equation:
i·ℏ·∂_t Ψ = [ −ℏ²/(2m)∇² + V_ext + Λ|Ψ|² + V_mem + α(−Δ)^(σ/2) − iΓ ]Ψ + η

The terms are presented here as a coupled system, showing how they interact — not as isolated pieces.

**NLS/Gross-Pitaevskii core (kinetic + Λ|Ψ|²).** Without the other terms, one has the focusing NLS (Λ<0 in the focusing/attraction convention), i∂_tΨ = −½∇²Ψ + Λ|Ψ|²Ψ, whose case is Gross-Pitaevskii's when there is a V_ext trap. Conserved invariants (in the absence of −iΓ, η, V_mem): mass/norm M = ∫|Ψ|², energy/Hamiltonian E = ∫ [½|∇Ψ|² + (Λ/2)|Ψ|⁴], and momentum. **Collapse/blow-up:** governed by the **virial identity** (variance) V(t) = ∫ |x|²|Ψ|² dx, whose second time derivative satisfies d²V/dt² = 8E (for critical nonlinearity), so that E<0 forces V(t)→0 in finite time → blow-up (Vlasov–Petrishchev–Talanov and **Zakharov–Glassey** criterion). The **critical dimension** for cubic nonlinearity is d=2 (L²-critical: exponent 1+4/d); d<2 subcritical (global existence), d>2 supercritical. In the L²-critical case there is Merle–Raphaël's log-log law for the blow-up rate. References: Colliander/Merle–Raphaël notes (math.utoronto.ca); "Enhancement of the Zakharov-Glassey's method for Blow-Up in NLS" (arXiv:2203.08522); Merle, "Blow-up Results of Virial Type for Zakharov Equations" (*CMP* 175(2)).

**Fractional Laplacian α(−Δ)^(σ/2) (fractional Schrödinger / Lévy dynamics).** Definition by Fourier multiplier: (−Δ)^s u = F^{-1}[ |ξ|^{2s} F(u) ] — in code, multiply by |k|^σ in Fourier space (with σ = 2s in the author's convention). Introduced by **Laskin** (2000, *Phys. Lett. A* 268:298–305; 2002, *Phys. Rev. E* 66:056108) extending Feynman's path integral from Brownian to Lévy trajectories. Collapse effect: for focusing fractional NLS i∂_t u = (−Δ)^s u − |u|^{2p}u, **Boulenger, Himmelsbach & Lenzmann**'s general blow-up criterion holds for p ≥ 2s/N (radial, N≥2); the L²-critical case is p = 2s/N. Smaller s (more "fractional") reduces dispersion so as to alter the collapse threshold. Fractional↔cubic interaction with power combinations: Feng (2018, arXiv:1804.00973 and *CPAA*), "On the blow-up solutions for the fractional NLS with combined power-type nonlinearities", 0<p₁<p₂<2s/(N−2s), with sharp mass threshold. *Open* result preserved: a general blow-up existence theorem for local FNLS remains an open problem, supported by numerical evidence (cited in arXiv:1804.00973). FNLS as a boson-star/semi-relativistic model when s=1/2.

**Dissipative/absorbing term −iΓ (non-Hermitian).** Makes the Hamiltonian non-Hermitian; complex potentials model gain/loss. **PT-symmetry**: a PT-symmetric Hamiltonian can have real spectrum despite being non-Hermitian; in photonics, complex potentials = media with localized amplification/absorption. Relation to **Lindblad master equations**: an effective non-Hermitian Hamiltonian emerges from open-system Lindblad dynamics (the −iΓ term as single-particle loss, ρ_{11}(t)=ρ_{11}(0)e^{−2γt}). **Complex absorbing potentials (CAP)** are used to absorb the wave function at the boundaries. Relation to blow-up prevention: dissipation/absorption (Γ>0) drains norm and energy, potentially preventing explosion. References: El-Ganainy et al. (2018), "Non-Hermitian physics and PT symmetry" (*Nat. Phys.* 14:11); "Density Matrix Formalism for PT-Symmetric Non-Hermitian Hamiltonians with the Lindblad Equation" (arXiv:2006.02445); "Dissipative Dynamics and Active Stabilization of Linear and Nonlinear Waves in Non-PT-Symmetric Harmonic Traps" (arXiv:2605.12638, 2026), which explicitly treats active stabilization of nonlinear waves via imaginary potential. For damped fractional NLS: "Global existence for the nonlinear fractional Schrödinger equation with fractional dissipation" (arXiv:1704.05307) — global existence for any damping term a>0.

**Stochastic forcing η (stochastic NLS).** The **de Bouard & Debussche** literature is central and contains both positive and contradictory results preserved here:
- Multiplicative noise (white in time, correlated in space) has the effect of *delaying* blow-up; white noise can even *prevent* blow-up (observations of de Bouard, Debussche & Di Menza, 2001, *Journées EDP*).
- However, de Bouard & Debussche (2005, *Ann. Probab.* 33(3):1078–1110), "Blow-up for the stochastic NLS with multiplicative noise": in the supercritical case, spatially smooth noise can *cause* immediate blow-up with positive probability for any smooth initial data.
- *Conservative* (Stratonovich) noise in the L²-supercritical case *accelerates* blow-up with positive probability; large *non-conservative* (Gaussian multiplicative) noise can *prevent* blow-up on any bounded interval with high probability (Barbu, Röckner & Zhang; see arXiv:2210.17010 and *Nonlinear Analysis* 136:102–116, 2016). Additive effects: de Bouard & Debussche (2002, *Probab. Theory Relat. Fields* 123:76–96).
- Foundations: de Bouard & Debussche (1999, *CMP* 205:161; 2003, *Stoch. Anal. Appl.* 21:97) — well-posedness in L² and H¹ via stochastic Strichartz estimates. Stochastic fractional case: "Stochastic Perturbations in the Fractional NLS: Well-posedness and Blow-up" (arXiv:2308.10270) — notes that the classical variance-virial law *fails* in the fractional scenario, requiring stochastic generalization via Itô's formula. Energy-critical case: arXiv:2604.14852 (2026). "Stochastic Schrödinger equations"/quantum state diffusion (Fagnola & Mora, 2013) connect η to quantum state diffusion.

**Memory term V_mem (history-dependent potential).** Literature forms for implementing history dependence:
- **Non-instantaneous response (Raman-type in optics):** GNLSE with R(t) = (1−f_R)δ(t) + f_R h_R(t), where the δ term is instantaneous Kerr and the h_R convolution is memory. Blow–Wood kernel: h_R(t) = Θ(t)·(τ₁²+τ₂²)/(τ₁τ₂²)·e^{−t/τ₂}·sin(t/τ₁), with f_R=0.18, τ₁=12.2 fs, τ₂=32 fs for silica (Blow & Wood, 1989, *IEEE JQE* 25(12):2665; Lin & Agrawal, 2006, *Opt. Lett.* 31(21):3086; Agrawal, *Nonlinear Fiber Optics*, 6th ed.).
- **Integro-differential equation with convolution kernel:** i∂_tΨ = H₀Ψ + ∫₀^t K(t−t′)Ψ(t′)dt′. Non-stationary kernels: "Exact treatment of the memory kernel under time-dependent system-environment coupling" (arXiv:2505.00386). Time-nonlocal potentials in Schrödinger via Laplace/Green (arXiv:2502.18700).
- **Fractional time derivative (time-fractional Schrödinger):** Naber (2004, *J. Math. Phys.* 45(8):3339; arXiv:math-ph/0410028) — replaces ∂_t with the Caputo derivative of order α; the Hamiltonian becomes non-Hermitian and time-nonlocal; solutions in Mittag-Leffler functions; *probability is not conserved* (preserved result). Caputo derivative: ₀^C D_t^α f = 1/Γ(1−α) ∫₀^t f′(τ)(t−τ)^{−α} dτ for α∈(0,1).
- **Data-driven kernel discovery** (if V_mem is learned): "Neural Discovery of Memory and Nonlocal Kernels in Integro-Differential Equations with Constrained KANs" (arXiv:2607.11110); MINPO (arXiv:2512.17273).

**How all terms interact (anti-collapse as a whole).** The material suggests that collapse vs global-existence behavior emerges from *joint competition*: focusing Λ|Ψ|² pushes toward blow-up; the kinetic and fractional α(−Δ)^(σ/2) disperse (with exponent σ adjusting the critical threshold); V_ext (trap) can confine; −iΓ drains norm/energy (dissipation preventing explosion); η can prevent/delay OR accelerate blow-up depending on whether it is non-conservative vs conservative multiplicative; V_mem adds a history term whose delayed response modifies instantaneous dynamics. *Known* anti-collapse mechanisms in the literature, presented as parallel material (not as replacement for the author's mechanism): nonlinearity saturation (e.g., cubic-quintic CQNLS — Nature *Sci. Rep.* 7285, where non-zero background interrupts collapse and generates dispersive shock waves), dissipation/absorption, non-Hermitian terms, stabilizing noise, traps, and nonlocal interactions (arXiv:1008.1891, "Collapse in the nonlocal NLS").

**Practical numerical methods for the complete equation.**
- **Split-step Fourier / pseudo-spectral (Strang, symmetric).** Write ∂_tΨ = (L+N)Ψ, L = linear part (kinetic + fractional + dissipative, diagonal in Fourier), N = nonlinear part (Λ|Ψ|² + V_ext + V_mem, diagonal in physical space). Symmetric O(Δt²) step: (i) linear half-step in Fourier Ψ̂ ← e^{iL̂Δt/2}Ψ̂; (ii) full nonlinear step in physical space Ψ ← e^{iN(|Ψ|²)Δt}Ψ; (iii) linear half-step. The **fractional Laplacian enters as the |k|^σ multiplier** in the Fourier linear operator: L̂ = −(ℏ/2m)|k|² − α|k|^σ − iΓ. For fractional NLS: Klein et al., time-splitting spectral (arXiv:1908.05719, harmonic potential); "A split-step Fourier pseudo-spectral method for space-fractional coupled NLS" (*Commun. Nonlinear Sci. Numer. Simul.*, ScienceDirect S1007570423000680); "A split-step finite element method for the space-fractional Schrödinger equation in 2D" (PMC11484848).
- **Stability and time step:** split-step numerical instability over a soliton background (Weideman–Herbst) requires attention to the step; see arXiv:1008.4974 and plane-wave stability analysis (arXiv:1306.0656). Symmetric scheme improves error to O(Δt²).
- **Memory term numerically:** discretization of the convolution integral by **L1 scheme** (Caputo, order 2−α): ∂̄_n^α φ = Σ_{j=1}^n b_{n−j}∂̄_jφ, with b_j = Δt^{1−α}/Γ(2−α)·[(j+1)^{1−α}−j^{1−α}] — O(N²) time cost, O(N) memory. **Sum-of-exponentials (SOE) approximation** by Jiang, Zhang, Zhang & Zhang (2017, *Commun. Comput. Phys.* 21(3):650–678; arXiv:1511.03453): approximates the t^{−1−α} kernel by Σ_ℓ w_ℓ e^{−s_ℓ t}, making convolution *recursive* (each mode y_ℓ^n = e^{−s_ℓΔt}y_ℓ^{n−1} + local quadrature), reducing to O(N·N_exp) work and O(N_exp) memory — fully differentiable (only exp/mult/sum). Higher-order schemes: L1-2, Alikhanov's L2-1σ (order 3−α); 4th-order compact in space (DOI 10.1186/s13662-020-02946-w).
- **Stochastic integrators (for η):** Euler–Maruyama (strong order 1/2 for general noise), Milstein and higher-order schemes for SPDEs; Itô vs Stratonovich interpretation matters (conservative noise is Stratonovich). de Bouard, Debussche & Di Menza give a numerical scheme with convergence proof for stochastic NLS.
- **Crank-Nicolson and relaxation schemes:** Besse's relaxation scheme preserves invariants in NLS; conservative CN-FDM for Riesz fractional NLS.
- **Exponential integrators** for the stiff linear part.
- **Joint treatment:** all terms in a single integrator via splitting: group {kinetic, fractional, −iΓ} in the Fourier operator (exact exponential), {Λ|Ψ|², V_ext} in the physical step, V_mem via SOE recurrence updated each step, and η injected by Euler–Maruyama in the physical step.
- **Libraries/codes:** **GPELab** (Antoine & Duboscq, 2014/2015, *Comput. Phys. Commun.* 185:2969 and 193:95; gpelab.math.cnrs.fr) — MATLAB, pseudo-spectral/relaxation/Crank-Nicolson, 1D/2D/3D GPE with local and nonlocal nonlinearities, multi-component, stochastic dynamics. **XMDS2** (Dennis, Hope & Johnsson, 2013, *Comput. Phys. Commun.* 184:201; arXiv:1204.4255; xmds.org) — XML→C++ code generator for stochastic SPDEs, spectral methods, adaptive RK, MPI. **TorchGPE** (Fioroni et al., 2024, *SciPost Phys. Codebases* 38; arXiv:2404.14401; github.com/qo-eth/TorchGPE) — symmetric split-step GPE/NLS solver in PyTorch, GPU, arbitrary time-dependent/self-consistent potentials; built on PyTorch's autograd FFT stack. **GPUE** (Schloss & O'Riordan, 2018, *JOSS* 3(32):1037), **spinor-GPE** (PyTorch), **BEC2HPC**.

### F) FIELD EQUATIONS AND CONTINUOUS DYNAMICS AS AI-MODEL SUBSTRATE

**Neural ODEs / SDEs / continuous-time RNNs.**
- **Neural ODEs** — Chen, Rubanova, Bettencourt & Duvenaud (2018, NeurIPS; arXiv:1806.07366): dz/dt = f(z,t,θ), backprop by adjoint method with O(1) memory.
- **Neural SDEs** — Li, Wong, Chen & Duvenaud (2020, AISTATS; arXiv:2001.01328), "Scalable Gradients for SDEs" — the basis of torchsde.
- **Continuous-time RNNs (CTRNN)** — Funahashi & Nakamura (1993).
- **Liquid Time-Constant networks (LTC)** — Hasani et al. (2021): dx/dt = −[1/τ + f(x,u,t)]x + f(x,u,t)A, input-dependent time-constants; **CfC** (Closed-form Continuous-time, Hasani et al. 2022) gives an analytic solution (~20× speedup); **Liquid-S4** integrates LTC with SSMs. LFM2 (arXiv:2511.23404) is a recent application.

**State-space models (SSMs) and continuous-time formulation.** S4 (Gu, Goel & Ré, 2021), S5, **Mamba/Mamba-2** (Gu & Dao, 2023/2024): structured linear recurrence; continuous time is discretized by zero-order hold (ZOH), equivalent to Euler at first order (arXiv, "Mamba neural operator"; "Demystifying the Token Dynamics of Deep Selective State Space Models", arXiv:2410.03292). Relevant as substrate because it connects continuous dynamics ↔ sequence, and because H-Net's encoders/decoders use SSMs.

**PINNs and operator learning.** Physics-based networks (PINNs) and operator learning: **Fourier Neural Operator (FNO)** (Li et al., 2020) and **DeepONet** (Lu et al., 2021) learn operators between function spaces — a way of learning the field/PDE operator. "Mamba neural operator" (ScienceDirect S0021999125008496) generalizes SSM to neural operators for PDE families.

**Hamiltonian/quantum dynamics as network core.** **Hamiltonian Neural Networks** — Greydanus, Dzamba & Yosinski (2019, NeurIPS; arXiv:1906.01563): learns H(q,p) with loss L = ½[(∂H/∂p − dq/dt)² + (∂H/∂q + dp/dt)²], conserving energy and being time-reversible (code: github.com/greydanus/hamiltonian-nn). Related: Lagrangian Neural Networks (Cranmer et al., 2020, arXiv:2003.04630), Symplectic RNN (Chen et al., 2020, arXiv:1909.13334), Hamiltonian Generative Networks (Toth et al., 2020, arXiv:1909.13789). HNNs are a subclass of Neural ODEs (the learned H defines the vector field integrated by a symplectic solver). Unitary Evolution RNN (Arjovsky, Shah & Bengio, 2016, ICML; arXiv:1511.06464) — unitary dynamics preserving phase, avoiding vanishing/exploding gradients.

**Complex and wave-based representations.**
- **Deep Complex Networks** — Trabelsi, Bilaniuk, Zhang, Serdyuk, Subramanian, Santos, Mehri, Rostamzadeh, Bengio & Pal (2018, ICLR; arXiv:1705.09792): complex convolution, complex batch-norm (whitening via 2×2 real/imag covariance matrix), Rayleigh initialization, complex activations (modReLU, CReLU). Code: github.com/ChihebTrabelsi/deep_complex_networks.
- **Complex backprop / Wirtinger calculus** — operators ∂/∂z = ½(∂/∂x − i∂/∂y), ∂/∂z* = ½(∂/∂x + i∂/∂y). For real loss L, the descent direction uses the *conjugate* derivative ∂L/∂z* (the convention adopted by PyTorch's complex autograd). Non-holomorphic chain rule: ∂L/∂z* = (∂L/∂w)(∂w/∂z*) + (∂L/∂w*)(∂w*/∂z*). Refs: Brandwood (1983, *IEE Proc. F* 130(1):11), Kreutz-Delgado (arXiv:0906.4835), survey arXiv:2101.12249, Hirose (2012, Springer).
- **Phase as information carrier:** Reichert & Serre (2014, ICLR; arXiv:1312.6115), "Neuronal Synchrony in Complex-Valued Deep Networks".
- **Holographic Reduced Representations (HRR)** — Plate (1995) — binding by circular convolution. **Vector Symbolic Architectures / Hyperdimensional Computing** (Kanerva) — binding/bundling in high-dimensional vectors; connection to distributed representations and phase.

**Representation collapse (parallel technical reference — does not replace the author's mechanism).** The homonymous "collapse" phenomena in ML: **representation collapse** (features collapse to a single value, variance→0), **dimensional collapse** (representations do not use the whole space; measured by covariance singular values), **informational collapse** (correlated features), **rank collapse** in transformers. Known anti-collapse mechanisms: **VICReg** (Bardes, Ponce & LeCun, 2022) — variance terms (hinge over standard deviation ≥ γ), invariance and covariance (minimizes off-diagonals); **Barlow Twins** (Zbontar et al., 2021) — forces the cross-correlation matrix to identity ("soft whitening"); **whitening** (Ermolov et al., 2021); **spectral / orthogonal** weight regularization (WERank, arXiv:2402.09586). Style note: presented as parallel material; the name "collapse" here does not presume equivalence with the "collapse" the author's equation fights.

### G) ENGINEERING AND IMPLEMENTATION

**Coupling a PDE/field solver inside a differentiable pipeline.**
- **Adjoint method (continuous / optimize-then-discretize):** derive the adjoint ODE a(t)=∂L/∂z(t), da/dt = −a(t)ᵀ∂f/∂z (integrated backward), dL/dθ = −∫ a(t)ᵀ∂f/∂θ dt; O(1) memory but *approximate* gradients (reverse reconstruction accumulates error). It is Diffrax's `BacksolveAdjoint`, labeled "not recommended" for inaccuracy. Theoretical roots: Pontryagin et al. (1961).
- **Backprop-through-the-solver (discretize-then-optimize):** differentiates the solver's actual discrete operations → *exact* gradients, larger memory, mitigated by **checkpointing** (Griewank & Walther, "Algorithm 799: revolve", *ACM TOMS* 2000; ANODE, Gholami, Keutzer & Biros, IJCAI 2019, arXiv:1902.10298; Adaptive Checkpoint Adjoint, Zhuang et al., ICML 2020; Symplectic Adjoint, Matsubara, Miyatake & Yaguchi, NeurIPS 2021, arXiv:2102.09750). It is `RecursiveCheckpointAdjoint` (Diffrax's default). For split-step Fourier, discretize-then-optimize is recommended (exact gradients) with checkpointing — the FFT is a unitary linear operator whose VJP is another (conjugate) FFT, so gradients flow cleanly through `fft`/`ifft` + pointwise multiplications. See also McCallum et al. (arXiv:2410.11648, "Efficient, Accurate and Stable Gradients for Neural ODEs") and Kidger (DPhil thesis, Oxford, 2021, "On Neural Differential Equations").

**Frameworks.**
- **PyTorch `torch.fft`** (since 1.8): autograd + GPU (cuFFT). *Caveat*: complex autograd uses the Wirtinger-conjugate convention (stored grad is ∂L/∂z*); reduce to a real scalar before `.backward()` (else RuntimeError).
- **JAX** (`jax.numpy.fft`, `jax.lax`): differentiable, XLA GPU/TPU; composes with `grad`/`vjp`.
- **diffrax** (Kidger, JAX; github.com/patrick-kidger/diffrax, docs.kidger.site/diffrax) — ODE/SDE/CDE, multiple adjoints (`RecursiveCheckpointAdjoint`, `BacksolveAdjoint`, `ImplicitAdjoint`); built on Equinox, Python 3.10+.
- **torchdiffeq** (github.com/rtqichen/torchdiffeq) — `odeint`/`odeint_adjoint` (adjoint requires `func` as `nn.Module`); dopri5/dopri8/bosh3/adaptive/implicit Adams solvers.
- **torchsde** (google-research/torchsde) — GPU SDE, reversible Heun.
- **torchcde** (Kidger) — Neural CDEs; **torchode** (Lienen & Günnemann, arXiv:2210.12375) — batched/JIT ODE problems; **TorchDyn** (Poli et al.).
- **Custom CUDA kernels / GPU FFT** for very-high-rate byte channels.

**Scale, memory and parallelism for very-high-rate byte channels.** The BLT/H-Net/MBLM pattern — concentrating most capacity in the **main network** operating over *compressed* sequences (L_S ≪ L_0), keeping lightweight byte-level encoders/decoders — is the direct strategy for very high rates: allocate computation where data are complex (entropy patching) and use SSMs (linear cost) at byte levels. Selective checkpointing (MBLM: up to 5M bytes on one GPU) and patch-batch packing (BLT) control memory peaks. For the field solver, split-step Fourier is dominated by GPU-parallelizable O(N log N) FFTs; the V_mem SOE recurrence keeps history memory O(N_exp) instead of O(N).

## Recommendations

Concrete, staged steps to gather/operationalize the technical material (respecting that the author's declared elements are not alterable):

1. **BYTE channel (ingestion by active inference).** Start from Buckley et al.'s (2017) continuous-time predictive-coding formulation and/or pymdp for F-only mode (without the G/action branch). Readiness benchmark: the system updates beliefs q(s) reducing F over a synthetic byte flow without ever emitting action. If the byte rate exceeds iterative-inference capacity, migrate to amortized inference (recognition network q_φ) and/or Neural Kalman Filtering.

2. **Byte↔token bridge (learning computation in [0,1]).** Instantiate the continuous value per hard-concrete gate (Louizos et al.) or Gaussian stochastic gate (Yamada et al.), with α/μ parameters learned online by gradient — this satisfies "learning the number as it goes". For the "how much was learned" signal driving that value, use Bayesian surprise (Itti & Baldi — D_KL[posterior‖prior]) or compression progress (Schmidhuber), aware of the negative result of arXiv:2605.14831 (bias in general MDPs). Decision-changing threshold: if the learned value saturates at 0 or 1 for all items (gate collapse), reintroduce temperature/stretching (ζ,γ) or switch to lower-variance Gaussian STG.

3. **Field equation (solver as a whole).** Implement symmetric split-step Fourier with: Fourier operator L̂ = −(ℏ/2m)|k|² − α|k|^σ − iΓ (fractional via |k|^σ, dissipation via −iΓ); physical step with Λ|Ψ|² + V_ext; V_mem by sum-of-exponentials recurrence (Jiang–Zhang et al.); η by Euler–Maruyama (Itô or Stratonovich per the noise's nature). Code base: start from TorchGPE (PyTorch, autograd-capable) and extend with the fractional term, −iΓ, V_mem and η. Benchmark: numerically reproduce the known regimes (L²-critical blow-up without the stabilizing terms; blow-up prevention/delay when switching on −iΓ and/or non-conservative multiplicative η, per de Bouard–Debussche). Threshold: if step Δt triggers Weideman–Herbst instability over a soliton background, reduce Δt or use an exponential integrator on the stiff part.

4. **Differentiability.** Use discretize-then-optimize (backprop through the FFT) with recursive checkpointing for exact gradients through the solver; reserve continuous adjoint (backsolve) only if memory is the dominant bottleneck, aware of inaccuracy. Validate gradients by numerical check (finite differences) on a small case, minding PyTorch's Wirtinger-conjugate convention.

5. **Any-to-any multimodality.** For the TOKEN channel (I/O), the material on modality-specific tokenizers → discrete tokens (4M-21) and diffusion+autoregression mixture (Transfusion) provides the projection and output patterns; multimodal routing/MoE to direct modalities. Keep lightweight byte-level encoders/decoders and the backbone over compressed representations (BLT/H-Net pattern).

General benchmarks/thresholds changing the choices: (i) if entropy patching produces degenerate patches (all size 1 or all giant), recalibrate the entropy model or migrate to H-Net's differentiable router; (ii) if the BYTE channel does not converge in F, revise precision weighting; (iii) if the field solver diverges numerically even with dissipative terms, revise the stochastic interpretation (Itô vs Stratonovich) and the memory scheme.

## Caveats

- **Homonymy does not imply equivalence.** All literature terms here — "collapse" (blow-up in NLS; representation/dimensional/rank collapse in ML; wave-function collapse in QM), "memory" (V_mem, Raman response, Caputo, EWC/SI/MAS, SSM memory), "field", "atom", "token", "learning" — are presented as available technical material, NOT as definitions of what the author meant. They are distinct concepts sharing names.
- **Negative/contradictory results preserved.** The effect of η noise on blow-up is genuinely ambiguous in the literature: it can prevent/delay (non-conservative multiplicative) or accelerate/cause (conservative, or spatially smooth in the supercritical case) — both reported. EWC "consistently shows suboptimal performance". The classical variance-virial law *fails* in the fractional case. A general blow-up existence theorem for local FNLS remains open. The critical evaluation of predictive coding as a backprop alternative (Zahid et al. 2023) questions alleged advantages.
- **Variable-quality sources.** The central mathematical formulations come from peer-reviewed papers (Ann. Probab., CMP, J. Math. Phys., Comput. Phys. Commun., NeurIPS, ICLR, *Neural Computation*, *Vision Research*). Some secondary support points come from survey/blog pages (Medium, emergentmind) and were used only for contextualization, not for the central quantitative facts.
- **2026 dates and pre-prints.** Several items are 2025–2026 pre-prints (arXiv) not necessarily yet peer-reviewed (e.g., arXiv:2605.*, 2606.*, 2607.*); they are reported as recent available material, with the pre-print status caveat.
- **Non-calibration respected.** No recommendation suggests "taming" or linearizing the equation; the Λ|Ψ|² nonlinearity is treated as an element to respect. The numerical methods are for *solving* the equation as declared (all terms together), not for simplifying it.
- **TorchGPE.** Reusing TorchGPE as a solver base is an extension: the authors' paper emphasizes GPU acceleration, not end-to-end gradient training; differentiable use would need validation in implementation.
