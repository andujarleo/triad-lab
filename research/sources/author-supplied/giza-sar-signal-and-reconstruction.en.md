# Deep Research report: claims of structures beneath the Giza pyramids

Date: 2026-09-07

## Question

Verify the video's claims about Filippo Biondi, Corrado Malanga, SAR/Doppler, giant structures beneath the Giza pyramids, retraction of the 2022 paper, and comparison with accepted archaeological evidence.

## Direct answer

The claim of a city, eight cylinders, spirals, or artificial structures at 648 m or 2 km beneath the Khafre pyramid is not publicly demonstrated. The case has three layers:

1. Confirmed: Biondi and Malanga published a 2022 paper on Doppler SAR of the Great Pyramid/Khufu; that paper was retracted in 2026 by the journal Remote Sensing over methodological flaws and serious statistical errors.
2. Confirmed: the 2025 viral claim about Khafre was spread through a conference, websites, videos, and podcasts, but did not appear as a peer-reviewed paper with reproducible data and code.
3. Confirmed by independent research: known voids and structures exist in Egyptian pyramids, including the ScanPyramids Big Void in Khufu, but that was detected by muography and confirmed by multiple detectors, not by deep satellite SAR.

Conclusion: the hypothesis "there are local underground structures at Giza" is plausible in a general sense; the hypothesis "there is an artificial megastructure hundreds of meters or 2 km down, detected by satellite SAR" remains extraordinary, unreproduced, and technically very weak on the publicly available evidence.

| The pyramids were not tombs because "pharaohs were not buried there" | Oversimplified | Chambers, sarcophagi, funerary complexes, and Old Kingdom context support a funerary function, even when specific mummies were not found intact. |

## Technical points

SAR is real. COSMO-SkyMed, TerraSAR-X, Capella, and Umbra are real systems. The problem is not the radar's existence but the proposed inference. Spaceborne SAR normally measures microwave backscatter from the surface and from shallow interactions conditioned by wavelength, moisture, roughness, vegetation, angle, and resolution.

In the archaeological literature, even favorable SAR uses concern shallow structures, crop marks, subtle relief, paleochannels, moisture, and heritage monitoring. A recent geoarchaeology paper notes that L-band can penetrate up to about 24 cm under optimal dry-sand conditions, and that X-band, like COSMO-SkyMed, has limited penetration. That is several orders of magnitude away from 648 m or 2 km.

The theoretical escape of Biondi/Malanga is to say they are not simply "penetrating" with radar but reading micro-movements/vibrations of the target and turning that into a tomographic image. That leap is exactly where validation is missing: raw data, code, positive control, negative control, error, sensitivity, comparison with independent geophysics, and demonstration on a known target.

## What separates ScanPyramids from the Biondi/Malanga case

ScanPyramids is a good comparative control because it also investigated internal voids, but with a different evidence standard:

- physical technique suited to the problem: muography;
- detectors installed at different positions;
- confirmation by three instrumental families;
- publication in Nature and later Nature Communications;
- cautious dimensions: Big Void at least 30 m long; north-face corridor about 9 m by 2 m x 2 m.

This shows that "there are still unexplained voids" is true. But it also shows the minimum standard expected before accepting a major discovery.

## Where the video is right

- Right to say SAR exists.
- Right to say there was a 2022 paper and later repercussion.
- Right to distinguish that ScanPyramids found real voids via muons.
- Right to note that proprietary method/closed code prevents independent reproduction.

## Where the video slips

- Mixes the real evidence of voids in Khufu with the far larger claim of megastructures beneath Khafre.
- Treats the retraction as if it opened a bigger mystery; technically, it lowers confidence in the method sustaining the narrative.
- Suggests that "criticism after going viral" may be a discrediting modus operandi, but that is also exactly how science corrects extraordinary claims once they gain visibility.
- Uses "there are real underground structures" to keep a far larger-scale claim open. That is an undue leap: a known chamber, corridor, or shaft does not imply a megacity at 2 km.
- The line about the pyramids "having to have sunk" fails as a technical argument: they were built on the Giza rock plateau, and their stability requires no giant underground cylinders.
