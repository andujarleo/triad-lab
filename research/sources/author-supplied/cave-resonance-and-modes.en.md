Three distinct layers: what geometry does to sound, what was measured, and what that does not prove about intention. No text, no myth of a "universal sacred frequency".

1. What a pre-literate site can really do to sound

A stone cavity is a resonator. Mode frequency ~ speed of sound / 2L (or Helmholtz variants if there is throat + volume). In air at ~20°C, 110 Hz has a wavelength of ~3.1 m. Any chamber an adult fits into tends to have some mode in that band. That is physics, not evidence of design.

The useful test is not "does it resonate near 110 Hz?". It is: is the spectrum narrow and repeated in non-contiguous chambers? Does the geometry need fine-tuning on several walls at once? Does the human voice (not a loudspeaker) couple and propagate the mode through the whole complex?

2. The four sites

Hal Saflieni (Malta, ~3600–2500 BC) — the strongest case

The only site intact enough for modern measurement. Three levels carved in globigerina limestone.

Measurements:

- Oracle Chamber: reported double resonance at ~70 Hz and 114 Hz; low male voice couples and drives the whole hypogeum. Skin drum drives via harmonics; shell/horn almost not at all.
- Till (2017, Antiquity): reverberation up to 16 s at 63 Hz; primary resonances measured around 41, 72, and 76 Hz — confirms a low effect, does not replicate all earlier numbers.
- Wolfe, Swanson & Till (2020, JAs Reports): the mid-level peak spectrum requires joint tuning of non-contiguous walls in independent chambers. The peaks are nearly equally spaced and approach a whole-tone scale (ratios ~9:8 and 10:9). That is the most serious statistical argument that the geometry is not accidental.

Xaghra (Gozo, circle + hypogeum): broad peak 25–34 Hz (infra + audible threshold), different from Saflieni. Not the same instrument.

Saflieni has no famous solar alignment. It is pure cavity.

Newgrange (Ireland, ~3200 BC) — the only one joining sun + chamber in the same volume

Solar fact: the roof-box admits the winter-solstice sunrise into the passage and lights the chamber's rear for minutes. That is established.

Acoustic fact: Jahn, Devereux & Ibison (1996, JASA) measured resonance ~110 Hz (95–120 Hz band in six British/Irish chambers, Newgrange included). Method: ear + SPL meter, no automated spectrum, pre-selected sample. Not replicated with a modern protocol in 30 years.

Later physical limits: Newgrange sustains standing waves; it does not generate strong Helmholtz — rounded stones and cairn cavities absorb. Rayleigh prediction for the dimensions ~ 109.7 Hz, which is expected for the size, not proof of tuning.

Maeshowe (Orkney) is the better pair: winter solstice (sunset) + very tight dry masonry + clearer Helmholtz. Newgrange is the most famous pair, not the acoustically "cleanest".

Stonehenge (~3000–2200 BC) — alignment yes, cavity no

Alignment: sunrise at summer solstice over the Heel Stone; opposite axis in winter. Real.

Acoustics: 1:12 model of the complete monument (157 stones, ~2200 BC), University of Salford (Cox, Fazenda, Greaney 2020):

- Mean reverberation time at mid frequencies: 0.64 ± 0.03 s
- Speech amplification inside the circle: 4.3 ± 0.9 dB
- No audible echoes; the inner stones scatter rather than forming a closed chamber
- Sound is not projected outward
- Authors' conclusion: unlikely that sound drove the stones' layout.

Bluestones ring when struck (lithophonia). That is material, not cavity.

Maltese hypogea beyond Saflieni

Xaghra and the small Santa Lucija hypogeum share form, not the same spectrum. Tarxien (above ground) has lows, but it is a temple, not an equivalent cavity. The "Maltese system" is negative architecture + surface temples; only Saflieni has the measurement set that survives scrutiny.

3. Paleolithic flutes — measured intervals, not "the scale"

Hohle Fels (Aurignacian, ≥35,000 cal BC)

Griffon-vulture radius, 21.8 cm preserved, 5 holes + V notch. Fine lines by the holes suggest marking before drilling. High manufacturing fact.

Replicas (same bone, different techniques) do not yield a single note set. One study playing HF1 as a clarinet (column closed at one end) reports:

| Hz | Approx. note | Step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 456 | Bb4 | half tone |
| 499 | B4 | half tone |
| 530 | C5 | half tone |
| 630 | Eb5 | whole tone |
| 707 | F5 | half tone |
| 834 | Ab5 | whole tone |
| 936 | Bb5 | — |

Deviations of −38 to +23 cents from equal temperament. Not modern diatonic.

As an edge flute (another reconstruction in the same paper): G5-A5-B5-D6-F#6-G6.

Pentatonic is a convenient description, not a single measurement. Breath pressure shifts pitch by tens of Hz.

What is solid: intentional instrumentation with calibrated holes, non-equal-chromatic scale, ambitus of about an octave + overblow.

Divje Babe I (~50–60 ka, Mousterian context)

Cave-bear cub femur, 2 complete holes + traces. Status still disputed: human perforation vs. carnivore tooth. The argument for (Turk, Dimkaroski) is positional + playable replicas. The argument against is taphonomy and uniqueness among hundreds of femurs at the site.

Replicas:

- Dimkaroski: 3/2 octaves, untempered, modern articulations possible — depends on reconstructing length, embouchure, and the 4th hole (thumb).
- Atema: "crushed" tetrachord, very low 4th (~411 cents), not clean diatonic.
- Fink (1997): diatonic fragment — requires an original length greater than the cub bone allows.

There are no "real intervals of the original". There are intervals of reconstructions. Treating Divje Babe as a measured flute skips the ontological step.

4. Solar alignment + resonant cavity at the same site

Real intersection, without forcing:

| Site | Sun | Cavity/resonance | Pair strength |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Newgrange | Winter solstice (sunrise) | Chamber + passage; ~110 Hz (1996, weak) | Only famous one |
| Maeshowe | Winter solstice (sunset) | Physically cleaner Helmholtz than Newgrange | Best pair |
| Loughcrew | Equinoxes | 95–120 Hz band in the 1996 study | Weak |
| Stonehenge | Solstices | Open circle, RT 0.64 s, no cavity | No |
| Hal Saflieni | Acoustics only | Best measured Neolithic resonator | — |

Newgrange is the only volume in which the same space receives the solar beam and was measured as a resonator. The coincidence is the site, not a demonstrated "circuit": nobody measured the chamber's mode while the beam enters. Newgrange's Helmholtz with the roof-box open drops to ~2 Hz (infra, not song). Different regimes.

5. What does not follow

- 110 Hz as a megalithic constant. Nearly every human chamber has a mode in that band. Cook et al. (2008) saw EEG change at 110 Hz (relative language deactivation, hemispheric shift) — small sample, pure tone in a lab, not song at the site. Does not validate "neural design."
- Conscious musical scale at Saflieni. The 2020 paper shows a spectrum resembling whole tones. Resembling ≠ a score.
- Flute = same grammar as the chamber. Hohle Fels is 30 thousand years and 1500 km from Newgrange. Cultural continuity is hypothesis, not datum.
