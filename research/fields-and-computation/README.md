# Fields and computation

**What can a field with history compute?**

What changes when computation has a continuing internal history? The supplied materials explore a field that receives inputs, evolves and responds, alongside neural fields and architectures that translate between forms of representation. This theme connects a concrete engineering question with TRIAD’s ontological reading of an active, coupled system.

The [author’s substrate note](../../docs/reference/concepts/substrate.md) asks how an incoming byte might participate in field evolution and how previous interaction might affect a later response. It is a proposal to investigate. The [two-channel field-computing report](../sources/author-supplied/two-channel-field-computing.md), lines 69, 143 and 193–203, develops representation and recursive history: its BYTE and TOKEN channels concern input granularity and interfaces. They are not two new TRIAD principles.

[Neural fields and Wilson–Cowan](../sources/author-supplied/neural-fields-and-wilson-cowan.md), lines 8–27, discusses interacting populations, spatial kernels and persistent patterns. This gives a technical comparison about local interaction and collective organization. The variables, kernels and assumptions belong to those models; a similar wave shape does not identify their dynamics with TRIAD.

The engineering report contains both useful proposals and prescriptions inconsistent with current project rules, including term removal. Its propagation recipe also has a sign inconsistency noted in the source catalog. The preserved report is research material, not an approved runner. New TRIAD work keeps P1, P2 and P3 together.
